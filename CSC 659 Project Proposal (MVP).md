## Motivation and Background

Often, people feel an urge to get away and travel, yet they frequently lack a specific destination in mind. This is true for office workers planning their annual leave, or students looking forward to their summer or winter breaks. However, going on a trip without a clear plan is risky. The hassle of dealing with details like weather, attractions, and expenses can quickly dampen one's enthusiasm for travel.

Our concept is a travel assistant powered by AI's web searching capabilities. It is a simple command line script that sends a travel planning request to the AI and saves its response, so we can test how well the AI helps plan trips and gives users the information they need without having to manually browse through countless websites.

While this idea uses the strong search power of AI, the internet is often cluttered with outdated or inaccurate data, such as businesses that have long since closed or tourist attractions that are no longer open. There is a real risk that an AI might put this old information into a travel itinerary. Because of this, the main focus of this project is evaluating the accuracy, reliability, transparency, and trustworthiness of the travel recommendations generated by the AI.

We found a research paper that closely aligns with our project concept. In 2024, Singh et al. introduced *TravelPlanner+*, a benchmark for personalized travel planning that incorporates user profiles and preferences into Large Language Model (LLM) agents. Their study compared personalized travel plans with generic ones and found that personalized plans were more frequently preferred in LLM based evaluations. However, the study also found that personalization did not always improve the ability of travel plans to satisfy planning constraints.

Our approach is simpler than the one presented in this study. We believe that users should keep control over their travel decisions, while AI should serve as an assistant rather than a decision maker. When users already have a destination in mind, the planner will help them organize their trip and generate an itinerary based on their preferences and constraints. When users are unsure where to travel, the AI will suggest possible destinations based on their interests, budget, and available time, leaving the final decision to the user.

**Reference:** Singh, H., Verma, N., Wang, Y., Bharadwaj, M., Fashandi, H., Ferreira, K., & Lee, C. (2024). Personal Large Language Model Agents: A Case Study on Tailored Travel Planners. *Proceedings of the 2024 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing: Industry Track*, 486–514. https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-industry.37/

## Evaluation

Since travel preferences are subjective, there is no single standard for deciding the best travel plan. Our planner serves as a tool that gives suggestions, while the final decision always rests with the user. Because of this, our evaluation will focus on the accuracy, reliability, and practicality of the information provided by the AI. We plan to assess the planner based on four key criteria:

**Location:** The recommended destinations should be consistent with the user's geographical and time constraints. For example, if a user lives in San Francisco and wants a road trip that can be completed within a week, the AI should recommend destinations that are realistically reachable within that timeframe. Suggestions that break the user's stated travel distance or geographical limits would be considered inadequate.

**Budget:** The planner should give reasonable estimates for expenses, mainly accommodations and tourist attractions. Since these estimates are gathered from online sources, we will compare selected prices against official websites or other reliable sources to decide whether the AI's estimates are reasonably accurate. The planner should also avoid recommending options that go well over the user's stated budget.

**Climate:** The planner should consider whether the weather at a destination is suitable for travel during the user's planned dates. For trips in the near future, the AI should consider available weather forecasts. For trips planned further in advance, it should rely on historical climate data to identify typical seasonal conditions. When bad weather is likely, the planner should warn the user about the risks rather than presenting the destination as an ideal choice.

**Attractions:** The planner should check that recommended tourist attractions and businesses are still operating. We will check selected recommendations against official websites or other reliable sources to find permanently closed businesses, seasonal closures, and other availability limits. Recommending attractions that are no longer open without telling the user would be considered an error.

### Evaluation Method

Our evaluation will focus on verifying the accuracy and reliability of the information provided by the AI. Each of our four team members will run 30 independent tests, for a total of 120 experiments.

For each experiment, a team member will run the script with a travel planning request and read the recommendations generated by the AI. We will verify the information using official websites and other reliable online sources, focusing on four criteria: location, budget, climate, and attractions.

Each applicable criterion will be marked as Pass or Fail based on whether the information is accurate and meets the user's requirements. We will record the results of all 120 experiments in our spreadsheet and calculate the overall pass rate for each criterion. This will show us which types of travel information the AI handles reliably and where mistakes are most likely to happen.
